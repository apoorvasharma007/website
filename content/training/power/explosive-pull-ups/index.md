An explosive pull-up is a regular pull-up with one rule changed: pull as fast as you can, every rep. The goal stops being "get your chin over the bar" and becomes "get there with speed to spare." That spare speed is what eventually turns into a muscle-up.

> [!note] Prerequisites
> Around 8–10 clean strict pull-ups before making explosive work a focus. Below that, strict volume builds power faster than power work does.

## Why train them

Strength is how much force you can produce. Power is how fast you can produce it. A slow grinding pull-up and an explosive one use the same muscles, but they train different qualities — and everything on the bar that looks impressive (muscle-ups, bar transitions) lives on the power side.

For me they are the bridge exercise: the direct line from "can do pull-ups" to the muscle-up family I'm working through.

## How I progress them

Each step is the same movement with more height or less contact:

1. **Speed pull-ups** — strict form, maximum intent on every concentric, slow controlled negative.
2. **Chest-to-bar** — same pull, higher target.
3. **Waist-to-bar** — the pull that makes muscle-ups possible.
4. **Release at the top** — hands briefly leave the bar. Start small.
5. **Clap pull-ups** — release plus a clap. Mostly a party trick, but an honest test of height.

Move to the next step when you can do about 5 crisp reps at the current one. Crisp matters more than five — the first slow rep of a set is the last rep of that set.

> [!warning]
> Explosive reps load the shoulders harder than strict ones, especially on the catch after a release. Warm up the shoulders properly, keep sets short, and stop the session when height drops — grinding through fatigue trains slowness.

## How I program them

Power work goes first in the session, after warm-up and before strength work, while the nervous system is fresh.

| Variable | What I do |
|---|---|
| Sets × reps | 4–6 × 3–5 |
| Rest | 2–3 min, full recovery |
| Frequency | 2×/week |
| Intensity cue | Every rep max speed; end the set when speed drops |

That's it. Low reps, long rest, high intent. It feels like you're not doing enough — that's the point.
